Before you buy, focus on the car's history, condition, and total cost—not just the asking price. Set an all-in budget including sales tax, registration, insurance, fuel, repairs, tires, and any loan interest. Check the VIN history for salvage or rebuilt title, flood damage, major accidents, odometer discrepancies, liens, recalls, and ownership history. Get a pre-purchase inspection from an independent mechanic—not the seller's shop. Test-drive it properly: start it cold, drive city streets and highway speeds, brake firmly, and check steering, shifts, HVAC, electronics, warning lights, and odd noises. Look for maintenance records. Research the exact year, engine, and transmission for known expensive failures. Avoid title and paperwork surprises. Don't negotiate from emotion, and be wary of unusually cheap cars. If financing, arrange it first. If the seller refuses an inspection or won't provide the VIN, walk.
